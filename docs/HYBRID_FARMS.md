The approved model preserves village tool support while giving farms sales income:
contribute a capped share of future basic food toward actual meals/reserves, own
remaining accepted food and grain, and sell through real paid transactions. Old
public food, public grain and historical reports never change owners. Collective
village food purchases pay existing producers, with delayed owned cargo and the
existing real budget/need/qualification/world checks. No individual wallets or
salary system is introduced.

Newly generated economy.yml contains:

```yaml
farm-market:
  enabled: true
  village-share-percent: 50
  food-reserve-periods: 2
  storage-capacity: 256
  grain-stock-target: 16
```

Missing old sections opt out and installed files are never rewritten. Configure
actual farm businesses as well; legacy unassigned field accounting cannot create
private surplus. Grain uses the separately editable grain-harvest rates/yield,
typed wheat share and meal protection. The new target limits unsold ingredients
so they cannot crowd every food unit out of commercial storage. Existing excess
stock is retained when targets are lowered; no spoilage or unexplained deletion.

Contributions cover only the gap to this period's actual meals plus future
reserves, after counting public food and genuinely due imports. Their percentage
cap applies to actual accepted basic food production, even when the town needs
more. A farm cannot automatically donate its whole harvest to fill an impossible
need. Village customers can pay for more, drawing from the farm's owned goods.
If cash or production is insufficient, a real shortage remains. Growth/expansion
and wider food-sector acceptance still need integration/testing; no guaranteed
infinite food or treasury is promised.

One actual harvest budget is partitioned into public food, owned basic food and
grain work. Excess work that cannot fit owned storage pauses; it is not added to
donations or made into free goods. Tool service and farming experience follow
actual accepted work, including harvests with no new public food. One toolkit
slot is reserved beyond commercial capacity, and outstanding toolkit orders
count against the one-kit limit.

Food periods, commercial output, stock adoption, harvest proof and checkpoint
commit together. Proof retains actual/potential work, allocation, source crop
composition/capture, percentage and grain yield/settings. Late failure rolls back
these rows. Existing successful business founding may precede a failed period;
retry retains that business without repeating startup spending.

Paid food orders credit the farm's existing business cash account. Protected public
tool invoices add exactly the invoice funding temporarily and spend it on the real
supplier, leaving earned farm cash intact. Actual grain/mixed harvest work now
qualifies consistently for those invoices; the earlier food-vs-total-work equality
could incorrectly block grain-producing farms. Goods cannot be purchased or
delivered twice. An absent world/server earns no production or learning.

Admin food diagnostics explain public contributions, owned food/grain, earned cash
and accepted/available work. Automatic livestock/bakery/craftsman founding and
